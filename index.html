Small samples are flagged, not hidden. Areas with fewer than 10 samples are marked on the map and in the table, so a single positive hive never reads as a regional outbreak. Exact apiary locations are never shown.

Where the data comes from

The Hive Atlas compiles published, openly available data. Every figure links back to its source, and the full methods are documented.

  • Disease diagnosticsAdult bee samples tested by the USDA Bee Disease Diagnostic Laboratory since 1984.
  • Colony and loss surveysUSDA NASS colony counts and published colony loss surveys.
  • Sequence repositoriesMitochondrial and genomic sequence data from public databases such as NCBI.
  • Peer-reviewed studiesDatasets published alongside research on honey bee pathogens and population genetics.

Have honey bee data to share?

Researchers, apiary inspectors, and beekeepers anywhere in the world can contribute data with full citation credit and privacy protections for apiary locations.
