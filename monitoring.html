    What this page does
    
            
    Colony monitoring programs inspect the same colonies again and again through the season, recording colony strength, brood, queens, Varroa, disease signs, and management. This page pools those inspections in two ways:
    
            
      
              
    • Regional view combines every inspection by climate region, so you can see how colonies typically develop through the year where you are and how regions differ.
      • 
              
    • Your report takes a file of your own inspections and places your numbers among comparable operations: the same region, the same type of operation, or both.
      • 
            
    
          
    

          
    
            
    Demonstration data
    
            
    Every number on this page is currently simulated. The records were generated by a script to show how the page will work, following broad seasonal patterns: colonies build up in spring and shrink in fall, earlier in warmer regions, Varroa counts rise through summer unless treated, and colonies with high fall mite loads are more likely to die over winter. They are not estimates of real colony health and should not be cited. They will be replaced with real monitoring data contributed by inspectors, researchers, and beekeepers.

    The measures
    
            
    Each inspection of a living colony counts once. Regional values need at least 20 inspections; fewer are striped on the map.
    
            
    
              
    
                
    MeasureHow it’s calculatedUnit
    
                
                  
    Varroa mites per 100 beesMites counted in an alcohol wash (or sugar roll) divided by bees sampled, times 100. A half cup of bees is about 300. Sticky-board counts are left out because they aren’t comparable.mites / 100 bees
    
                  
    Washes at or above 3Share of washes at or above 3 mites per 100 bees, a commonly used treatment threshold.% of washes
    
                  
    Frames of beesDeep-frame equivalents covered with bees.frames
    
                  
    Frames of broodDeep-frame equivalents with brood.frames
    
                  
    Solid brood patternShare of inspections scoring the brood pattern 4 or 5 on a 1 (very spotty) to 5 (solid) scale.% of inspections
    
                  
    QueenrightQueen seen, eggs present, or a virgin queen present.% of inspections
    
                  
    Disease signsShare of inspections where signs of American or European foulbrood, chalkbrood, or sacbrood were noted, plus deformed wings and small hive beetles separately. These are field observations, not lab-confirmed diagnoses.% of inspections
    
                  
    Treated, fedShare of inspections where a Varroa treatment or feeding was recorded.% of inspections
    
                  
    Winter colony lossColonies alive at an inspection between September and December that were found dead at their first inspection the next January to May. Colonies not inspected again are left out.% of colonies
    
                    
              
    
            
    
          
    

          
    
            
    Your report
    
            
    Your numbers are compared operation to operation. For each comparable operation, the page works out the same value you see for yourself, for example its average fall mite count, and then shows where you fall among them:
    
            
      
              
    • Typical peer is the median operation.
      • 
              
    • The shaded band is the middle half of operations, from the 25th to the 75th percentile, and each dot is one operation.
      • 
              
    • Better than most means you’re better than at least 75% of them, Worse than most means fewer than 25%, and anything between is Typical. Whether higher or lower is better is noted on each measure.
      • 
            
    
            
    An operation needs at least 5 inspections in the period (3 colonies for winter loss) to be counted, and a comparison needs at least 5 operations. If your group is too small, choose a broader one under Compare with.
    
            
    Colonies to check lists colonies with something flagged at their latest inspection, such as mites at or above 3 per 100 bees, a missing queen, or disease signs. Signs of American foulbrood should be reported to your state apiarist.
    
          
    

          
    
            
    Preparing a file
    
            
    The easiest way to start is the Excel template, which has dropdown lists for every coded column, a field guide, and standard methods for alcohol washes, counting frames, and scoring brood. A CSV template with just the header row is also available. Either way, save your records with one row per colony per inspection. Only the first three columns below are required; use whatever else you record. Column names are matched loosely, so “Date” or “Hive” work too.

    Privacy
    
            
    Files you open in Your report are read by your own browser. They are not uploaded, stored, or shared, and they disappear when you close or reload the page. To add your records to the regional data that others see, use the Contribute page, where location privacy and credit are agreed first.
    
          
    

          
    
            
    Limits to keep in mind
    
            
      
              
    • Who contributes shapes the picture. Monitored colonies are not a random sample. Programs that track colonies closely may manage them better than average.
      • 
              
    • Methods vary. Frames of bees and brood patterns are judged by eye, and different inspectors score them differently.
      • 
              
    • Regions are broad. Climate regions group states with similar climates, but forage, elevation, and local weather vary a lot within them.
      • 
              
    • Treatment response is a rough check. The drop in mites after treatment depends on the product, timing, and how much brood was present. It isn’t an efficacy test.
