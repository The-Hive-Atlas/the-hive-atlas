About

Why The Hive Atlas exists

Honey bee health data is collected by federal programs, universities, state inspectors, and beekeepers across the country, but it is scattered across reports, papers, and spreadsheets. The Hive Atlas brings it together in one place that anyone can explore.

What guides the project

Three commitments shape every decision on the site.

Open

Data is free to view and download, and every figure links back to where it came from.

Careful

Measures are clearly defined, small samples are flagged, and methods are documented in full.

Private

Apiary locations and beekeeper identities are never shown, at any level of detail.

Who runs it

The Hive Atlas was founded by two honey bee scientists who have spent their careers on the diagnostics, genetics, and health of managed colonies.

MA

Mohamed Alburaki, Ph.D.

Co-founder

A research entomologist who studies how biotic and abiotic stressors affect honey bee health, the causes of queen failure, and how genetic background shapes colony behavior and disease. He earned his Ph.D. at Pierre and Marie Curie University in Paris and completed postdoctoral research on pesticide exposure and stress responses at Laval University, the University of Tennessee, and the University of Southern Mississippi.

SM

Shayne Madella

Co-founder

A molecular biologist with more than a decade of experience in honey bee disease diagnostics, mitochondrial haplotyping, and population genetics. Co-author of 13 peer-reviewed publications and a participant in international honey bee research working groups.
