About

Why The Hive Atlas exists

Honey bee health data is collected by federal programs, universities, state inspectors, and beekeepers across the country, but it is scattered across reports, papers, and spreadsheets. The Hive Atlas brings it together in one place that anyone can explore.

What guides the project

Three commitments shape every decision on the site.

Open

Data is free to view and download, and every figure links back to where it came from.

Careful

Measures are clearly defined, small samples are flagged, and methods are documented in full.

Private

Apiary locations and beekeeper identities are never shown, at any level of detail.

Who runs it

The Hive Atlas was founded by three honey bee scientists who have spent their careers on the diagnostics, genetics, epidemiology, and health of managed colonies.

Mohamed Alburaki, Ph.D.

Co-founder

A research entomologist who studies how biological and environmental stressors affect honey bee health, why queens fail, and how genetic background shapes colony behavior and disease. He earned his Ph.D. at Pierre and Marie Curie University in Paris, then did postdoctoral research on pesticide exposure and stress responses at Laval University, the University of Tennessee, and the University of Southern Mississippi.

Shayne Madella

Co-founder

A molecular biologist with more than a decade at the USDA Agricultural Research Service Bee Research Laboratory in Beltsville, Maryland, specializing in honey bee disease diagnostics, population genetics, and laboratory management. Before joining USDA, she managed the APHIS National Honey Bee Pests and Diseases Survey at the University of Maryland, where she also earned a B.S. in Cell Biology and Molecular Genetics. She is a U.S. Marine Corps veteran.

Nathalie Steinhauer, Ph.D.

Co-founder

A research entomologist who studies honey bee health from an epidemiological perspective, focusing on disease dynamics, surveillance, and monitoring. Using large-scale monitoring programs, citizen science, and field studies, she examines how management, biological stressors, and the environment interact to shape colony health. She earned her Ph.D. at the University of Maryland, served as Science and Research Officer for the Bee Informed Partnership, and now conducts research at Oregon State University.
